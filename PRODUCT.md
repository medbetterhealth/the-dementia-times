# The Dementia Times Product Context

## Register

brand

## Users

The primary readers are family caregivers, often older adults, who need practical dementia-care help while tired, worried, or dealing with an immediate problem. Secondary readers include clinicians, home-care professionals, care managers, and family members helping make care decisions.

## Product Purpose

The Dementia Times turns Dr. Erik Ilyayev's daily LinkedIn newsletter into a permanent, searchable, multilingual publication. Readers should be able to describe a real-life problem in ordinary language, get a concise direct answer, and continue into the most relevant full newsletter and cited sources. Success means a first-time visitor can reach useful guidance in one choice from the homepage without understanding issue names, taxonomy, RSS, or archive structure.

## Brand Personality

Calm, authoritative, and humane. The publication should feel medically responsible and editorially rigorous while remaining plain-spoken enough for a stressed family caregiver.

## Anti-references

- A generic healthcare marketing page dominated by promotional cards and vague promises.
- A dense professional portal that assumes readers understand clinical terms or information architecture.
- A newspaper imitation that favors visual ceremony over findability and legibility.
- A search experience that sends visitors through repeated menus before showing an answer.

## Design Principles

1. One choice should lead to help. Homepage pathways open direct answers and matching articles, never another chooser.
2. Write for the question in the reader's head. Use caregiver language such as "How do I get Mom to shower?" alongside clinical terminology.
3. Lead with the answer, then offer depth. Show common questions, concise relevant excerpts, full articles, and sources in that order.
4. Preserve publication trust. Keep Dr. Erik's authorship, source links, dates, article media, medical context, and translated-page disclosure clear.
5. Make every core task comfortable for older readers. Favor large targets, strong contrast, explicit labels, predictable navigation, and low cognitive load.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.2 AA. Core navigation and search must work with keyboard and screen readers, touch targets should be at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels, text must remain readable at 200 percent zoom, and motion must respect reduced-motion preferences. English, Spanish, and Russian versions must offer equivalent navigation, search, sharing, and article access.
